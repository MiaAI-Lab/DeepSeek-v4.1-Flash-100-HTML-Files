A small defence of looking closely

by E. Marlowe · first published in the winter number, 1994

There is a particular kind of attention that has nothing to do with concentration. It is the attention you give to a cup on a table at the end of a long day — not because the cup is important, but because you have decided, for a moment, to let it be.

I have come to think of this as the : the ordinary object refusing to disappear. It is not a grand idea. It does not need to be. It only asks that we — that is, before habit has already named it and put it away.

We are told to look at the big things: the horizon, the catastrophe, the future. But the horizon is mostly a rumour. What we actually see, all day, is a series of small surfaces, most of them slightly dirty, all of them carrying the evidence of somebody’s hand.

My grandmother kept a drawer of . A key without a lock. Three buttons. A pencil sharpener with the blade gone. She opened it maybe twice a year. She never called it a collection.

Attention, in this sense, is not a skill. It is a willingness to be . You are doing something else; the light moves; you look up. That is the whole method. Everything else is just description.

And description, done patiently, becomes a kind of love. Not the loud kind. The kind that notices the crack in the cup, the chip on the rim, and decides — without any drama at all — that these are part of the cup now, and that the cup is still worth using.

So this is my small defence: look closely, at something that does not matter. Let it take longer than it should. The world will still be there when you finish, .
