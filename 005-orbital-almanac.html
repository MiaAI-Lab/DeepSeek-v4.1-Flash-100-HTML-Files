Issue 17 / The Long Way Around

Essay — Celestial mechanics

The long
way around.

On ellipses, patience, and the quiet arithmetic that keeps Tuesday from becoming Thursday.

Text by Imogen ValePlate drawn by the Almanac studio
Essaypp. 12—15

We are taught to draw a circle and then corrected, years later, by an ellipse. The correction is small enough to miss on a school page — a circle pressed gently between two fingers — but it contains the whole difference between a clock and a season.

Kepler did not set out to make the heavens strange. He set out to make them fit. The orbit of Mars refused every perfect arrangement he offered it, and in that refusal was a fact: the planet spends more time far from the Sun than near it. Speed is not constant. Nothing in the sky keeps office hours.

The long way around is not an error. It is the only route that closes.
