TITLE
Field Notes — A Sketchbook in Three Pages

PROMPT
Create a playful hand-drawn sketchbook page for a fictional design practice, "Field Notes, vol. 6". A warm paper spread with a faint blue grid, spiral binding, wobbly pencil borders, highlighter marks, sticky notes and annotation arrows. The left page is an inside cover with three tabs (Home, Studio, Contact), a row of pen-colour swatches, a "Turn the page" button and three draggable sticky notes. The right page shows a different low-fidelity wireframe for each tab: a hero with a big X-box and scribbled headline, a studio page with a portrait doodle and team boxes, and a contact page with an envelope and form fields. Everything should look drawn by a confident hand, not generated by a UI kit. Fully responsive, one standalone HTML file with inline CSS/JS/SVG, no external images or fonts.

DESIGN INTENT
The charm is in the imperfections: lines that do not quite meet, boxes that lean, arrows with too much personality. The palette is paper, blue ballpoint, red pencil, graphite and a single yellow highlighter. The layout is a real spread with a spine, and the interactivity is small and tactile — tabs, pens, notes, a page turn.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
- Hand-drawn borders using asymmetric border-radius and slightly rotated elements.
- Blue graph-paper background and a spiral binding built from CSS rings.
- SVG doodles for arrows, circles, underlines and a little face.
- Sticky notes with paper shadows and drag-and-drop via pointer events.
- Pen-colour swatches that change the sketch ink via CSS custom properties.
- Three page states with a short paper-turn animation and page counter.
- Highlighter swipe behind a heading using a skewed pseudo-element.

INTERACTION MODEL
Tabs switch between Home, Studio and Contact with a brief page-turn fade; "Turn the page" cycles through them. Pen swatches change the ink colour of every sketch line. Sticky notes can be dragged anywhere within the spread and are clamped to its bounds. Sticky notes and tabs are keyboard focusable; reduced-motion removes the page-turn animation but keeps all functionality.
