TITLE
The Last Light — A Film Noir Title Card

PROMPT
Create a cinematic film-noir title page for a fictional 1948 film called "The Last Light". Almost monochrome: black, smoke grey, bone white and one deep blood red. A huge high-contrast serif title dominates the frame, with "LIGHT" in italic and an outlined ghost duplicate offset behind it. A single warm spotlight rakes across the page and follows the pointer; venetian-blind shadows fall diagonally over the title; film grain, gate weave, and a faint projector flicker animate the whole frame. Include a studio credit, director and cast block, a red certification stamp, a runtime/rating line, and a bottom programme strip with three screening times. A "house lights" button dims the spotlight and toggles a brighter, colder version of the scene. Fully responsive, one standalone HTML file with inline CSS/JS, no external images or fonts.

DESIGN INTENT
A title card you could project. The composition is built like a frame: strong black margins, one shaft of light, the title placed slightly off-centre, and credits arranged like a studio contract. Texture does the period work — grain, scratches, weave, bloom — without a single image asset. Red appears only in the stamp and the smallest programme marks.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
- CSS conic and linear gradients composited into a raking spotlight, driven by pointer position via custom properties.
- Venetian-blind shadow overlay made from repeating linear gradients with perspective skew.
- Duplicated outlined title, italic serif accent and cinematic letter-spacing.
- Animated film grain, gate weave and projector flicker; occasional scratch line.
- Registration-like corner marks, red circular certification stamp and typewriter credit block.
- Programme strip with hairline rules and hoverable screening times.
- Accessible house-lights toggle that changes the scene and pauses the flicker.

INTERACTION MODEL
Moving the pointer sweeps the spotlight and subtly shifts the blind shadows. "House lights" toggles between the dark projection and a brighter intermission state with a colder beam. Screening times highlight on hover. Reduced-motion stops grain, weave and flicker; all text remains fully legible.
