TITLE
Brick Party — A Neon Arcade Cabinet

PROMPT
Create a playable neon brick-breaker game presented as a beautiful retro arcade cabinet called "BRICK PARTY". Dark arcade room, magenta and cyan neon, a marquee with chunky pixel-inspired lettering, a glass screen with scanlines, a scoreboard, and a small control panel with instructions. The game itself is complete and playable: a paddle, a ball, five rows of coloured bricks, score, three lives, win and lose overlays, and a start/restart button. Move with the mouse, touch or arrow keys. Add glowing particles when bricks break and a subtle screen shake. Fully responsive, one standalone HTML file with inline CSS/JS, no external images, fonts or libraries.

DESIGN INTENT
The cabinet is the artwork: an object you would find in the corner of a very stylish bar, glowing after midnight. The game is simple and responsive, with tactile neon bricks, a chrome-ish paddle and an addictive loop. Nostalgia is handled with restraint — pixel type, scanlines and glow, but a refined palette and clean layout.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
- Neon cabinet frame with marquee, screen bezel, speaker grille, joystick and button details drawn in CSS.
- Canvas game with rounded bricks, glow, ball trail and brick-break particles.
- Scanline, reflection and vignette overlays on the screen glass.
- Pixel-inspired monospaced typography and a vibrant magenta/cyan/yellow palette.
- HUD with score, high score, lives and level; overlays for start, win and game over.
- Screen shake and flash on collision; ambient cabinet glow.
- Responsive scaling of the entire cabinet while keeping the canvas aspect ratio.

INTERACTION MODEL
Click Start or press Space to launch the ball. Move the paddle with the pointer, touch or left/right arrow keys; the ball auto-launches after a life is lost. Clearing all bricks shows a win state with a replay button; losing three lives shows a game-over state. Score and high score update live. Pause with P. Reduced-motion removes shake and particles but keeps the game fully playable.
