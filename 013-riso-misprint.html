01 — Editorialp. 04

The risograph was never meant to be perfect. It was meant to be fast: a machine for schools, churches, flyers, zines. It drinks ink from a tube, prints one colour at a time, and asks you to come back when the drum has stopped spinning.

What made it beloved was the gap between passes. The paper shifts. The ink sits on top rather than sinking in. Every copy is a little different, and the difference is the point.
