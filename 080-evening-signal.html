THE CITY · A QUIET REVOLUTION

The city learns to listen.

After years of noise, a new programme asks residents to sit still for ten minutes and report what they heard. The results are unexpectedly moving.

By Mara Bell · Friday, 17 October
The east side at dusk, where the listening began. Photograph: The Signal.

The first session took place in a bus shelter on the east side of the city. Twelve people arrived, sat down, and were asked not to speak for ten minutes. At the end, they wrote down what they had heard.

“A radiator in a flat I have never been inside,” wrote one participant. “A river under the road,” wrote another. The notes were collected, typed and filed. Within a week there were hundreds.

The project was intended as a small civic experiment. It has become something closer to a portrait of the city — not the one on the postcards, but the one that hums and drips and sighs around us.

THE CITY · A QUIET REVOLUTION

The city learns to listen.

After years of noise, a new programme asks residents to sit still for ten minutes and report what they heard.

By Mara Bell · Friday, 17 October 2026

The first session took place in a bus shelter on the east side of the city. Twelve people arrived, sat down, and were asked not to speak for ten minutes. At the end, they wrote down what they had heard.

“A radiator in a flat I have never been inside,” wrote one participant. “A river under the road,” wrote another. “Someone practising the same four bars of a song, badly, and then getting them right.”

The notes were collected, typed and filed in a room above the library. Within a week there were hundreds. Within a month, the city council had received a request to make the experiment permanent.

“We expected complaints about noise,” said the organiser. “Instead, people kept telling us what they missed. The low hum of the old refrigerators. The bell on the bicycle. The particular sound of a street before it wakes up.”

The sessions now run every Thursday at 18:30. Bring nothing. Leave your phone in your pocket. Sit for ten minutes. Write down what you heard.
