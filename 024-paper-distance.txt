TITLE
Paper & Distance — A Study in Getting There

PROMPT
Design a quiet, tactile landing page for a fictional travel studio called "Paper & Distance". The aesthetic is folded paper: warm off-white and cream stock, indigo ink, a single vermilion accent, and soft shadows at every crease. The hero says "A simpler way to get there." in a large editorial serif with an italic indigo phrase, beside a hand-folded paper plane drawn in SVG, floating above a dotted flight path. Clicking "Launch the plane" sends it along a gentle arc; the message updates when it lands. Below, three folded-paper cards (Pack light / Go slowly / Write it down) can be unfolded to reveal a sentence; a route diagram with dashed lines and stamped stops closes the page. Fully responsive, one standalone HTML file with inline CSS/JS/SVG, no external assets.

DESIGN INTENT
Travel as an act of attention, not consumption. The page looks like something folded on a kitchen table: creases, paper edges, a stamp, a route drawn by hand. Indigo is the ink; vermilion is the one thing you would circle twice. There are no photographs — the imagination is asked to do the work.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
- SVG paper plane built from four folded facets with distinct paper tones and a soft projected shadow.
- CSS folded-paper cards with clipped corners, crease gradients and a 3D unfold interaction.
- Dotted flight path in SVG with an animated dash offset and stamped stop labels.
- Large serif display type with indigo italic accents and tracked monospaced wayfinding labels.
- Paper texture, faint fold lines across the page, and realistic offset shadows.
- Plane launch animation using keyframed transforms, plus a status line that announces arrival.
- Responsive stacking, with cards becoming a vertical stack and the plane scaling down.

INTERACTION MODEL
Clicking "Launch the plane" animates the plane along an arcing flight and changes the status line from "Ready on the table" to "Arrived — 3,482 km, lighter." Each folded card is a button; clicking unfolds it to reveal a short note, and clicking again folds it back. Route stops can be hovered to show their names. All interactions are keyboard accessible, and reduced-motion makes the plane appear at its destination without the journey.
